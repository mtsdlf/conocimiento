## 1. Formatos/cohorte-o-ritmo-propio.md

**Faltaba:** una revisión sobre finalización y apoyo social en cursos a ritmo propio y masivos, y cualquier comparación controlada entre cohorte y ritmo propio. El resumen decía que no había evidencia comparativa.

**Se encontró:**

- Reiser y Sullivan (1978), *Reducing the Number of Withdrawals in Personalized System of Instruction Courses*, ponencia, ERIC ED155983, https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED155983. [M].
  - «Sixty-two Arizona State University students enrolled in a political science introductory research course were randomly assigned to either a self-paced or an instructor-paced group.»
  - «Results show that 10 of the 30 students (33 percent) in the instructor-paced group and 21 of the 32 students (66 percent) in the self-paced group withdrew from the course. No significant differences were found between groups in attitudes or achievements.»
- Surber et al. (1976), *Self-Pacing Versus Pacing Requirements: Criterion Measures, Student Evaluations, and Retention*, ERIC ED137149, https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED137149. [M].
  - «The subjects included 148 students enrolled in an introductory college level child development course. One group of 74 students was randomly assigned to a self-paced section and another group of 74 students was assigned to an instructor-paced group.»
  - «even though the self-paced group procrastinated while the instructor-paced group worked at an even rate throughout the semester, both groups scored similarly [...] No differences were found in the number of units completed, final grade distribution, or course withdrawal rates.»
  - Mismo resultado en Morris et al. (1978), *Self-Pacing Versus Instructor-Pacing: Achievement, Evaluations, and Retention*, Journal of Educational Psychology, ERIC EJ187995 [M]: «The number of completed units, grade distributions, and course withdrawal rates were also similar.» Parece la versión publicada del mismo estudio; no se pudo confirmar y no se usó en la nota.
- Heller et al. (2019), *Do Tutors Make a Difference in Online Learning? A Comparative Study in Two Open Online Courses*, Open Praxis 11(3), DOI 10.5944/openpraxis.11.3.960, ERIC EJ1234936. [M].
  - «Two free fully online courses were offered by Peoples-uni [...] both as self-paced courses available any time and as courses run over four weeks with tutor-led discussions.»
  - «Numbers of discussion forum posts were greater in tutor-led than self-paced courses. Measured outcomes of certificates of completion, quiz completion and marks gained were very similar and not statistically significantly different between the tutor-led and the self-paced versions of either course.»
  - El resumen no dice cómo se asignó a los participantes; la nota no lo presenta como aleatorizado.
- Topolovec (s. f.; metadatos del PDF de marzo de 2018), *A comparison of self-paced and instructor-paced online courses: The interactive effects of course delivery mode and student characteristics*, repositorio de TU Delft, https://repository.tudelft.nl/file/File_7db986d2-106b-4a9c-bdd7-8bd6d4857e35. [L]. No figura revista ni revisión por pares.
  - «This study uses data from 35 different courses (8 courses ran twice, for a total of 43 runs) offered by Delft University of Technology that ran in 2016 and 2017 on the edX platform.»
  - «In total, 12 739 participants were included in the analysis [...] they indicated they intended to complete the course in the pre-survey».
  - «11.60% students passed the course in the self-paced courses, and 20.02% in instructor-paced courses.»
  - «In self-paced courses there are less students in a verified track (9.34% vs. 13.36%), with previous experience with completing an online course (64.88% vs. 69.16%), students are younger (30.94 vs. 33.56% years old), and slightly less educated (74.07% vs. 80.42% with "higher" education), which are all factors connected with lower success in MOOCs in general.»
  - Conclusión: «the relationship between the course mode (self-paced or instructor-paced) and the success in course is more complex than simply saying the self-paced mode is related to lower completion rates.»
  - Cita además a Rhode (2009) sobre interacción y a Southard et al. (2015), no leídos.
- Estrada-Molina y Fuentes-Cancell (2022), *El engagement y la deserción en los MOOCs: Revisión sistemática*, Comunicar 70, DOI 10.3916/C70-2022-09, https://www.revistacomunicar.com/pdf/70/c7009es.pdf. [L] (versión en castellano). Las páginas difieren entre el repositorio y la revista (111-124 en el pie del PDF).
  - «Se revisaron artículos de revistas indexadas en Scopus o en WoS aplicando el protocolo PRISMA. Al finalizar el protocolo se definió analizar 40 estudios.»
  - «Los resultados reflejan que las principales variables son: el diseño e-actividades; la motivación intrínseca y extrínseca y; la comunicación entre los estudiantes. Se ratifica que los principales retos para garantizar el engagement en los MOOCs son: la tutoría individualizada; la interactividad; y la retroalimentación.»
  - «En la última década los estudios del engagement y su relación con la deserción en los MOOCs, no son suficientes».
- De paso, sin usar en la nota: Spencer et al. (1980), REDEAL Research Report #11, Athabasca University, ERIC ED249347 [M]: «completion rates were substantially higher for paced package courses than for home study courses». Lim (2016), Distance Education, ERIC EJ1117837 [M], sobre demoras en cursos a ritmo propio: «The average length of time between assignment submissions was found to be most useful to predict final letter grade and withdrawal.» Reich y Ruipérez-Valiente (2019) ya están en [finalización de los MOOC](../../Tecnologia-educativa/Aprendizaje-en-linea/finalizacion-de-mooc.md), que la nota enlaza.

**Se cambió:** el resumen y el primer párrafo ya no dicen que no hay evidencia comparativa. Nueva sección «Lo que muestran las comparaciones», con los dos ensayos de los años 70 bajo `> [!IMPORTANT]` porque no coinciden, Heller et al., Topolovec y la revisión de Estrada-Molina y Fuentes-Cancell. Fuentes nuevas en `fuentes:`.
