## 1. Modelos-de-diseno/4c-id.md

**Faltaba:** cuándo se da cada tipo de información dentro del curso y cómo se retira la ayuda en cada clase de tareas.

**Se encontró:**

- Bastiaens, van Merriënboer y Hoogveld (2002), *A Design Methodology for Complex (E)-Learning*, AHRD Conference Proceedings, ERIC ED474326, https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED474326.pdf. [L] (8 p., OCR con errores menores; van Merriënboer es coautor).
  - Retiro de la ayuda: «Much support is given for learning tasks early in a task class, and no support is given for the final learning task in a task class. This process of diminishing support as learners acquire more expertise is called "scaffolding". It is repeated for each subsequent task class, yielding a sawtooth pattern of support throughout the whole course program.»
  - Tipos de ayuda: «Support can take the form of product-oriented or process-oriented support. For product-oriented support, part of the solution is provided to the learners as part of the learning task. For instance, one might ask students to study a case or to complete a partial solution [...] With process-oriented support, the learners are guided through the problem solving process. For instance, one may require students to successfully complete one problem solving phase before continuing to the next phase, or present them with "process worksheets" [...] lists of leading questions that guide them through a systematic process.»
  - Información de apoyo atada a clases: «supportive information is not coupled to individual learning tasks but to task classes. The supportive information for each subsequent task class is an addition to or an elaboration of the previous information».
  - Información justo a tiempo: «JIT information provides learners with the step-by-step knowledge ("how-to-instruction") [...] directions teachers or tutors typically give to their learners during practice, acting as an "assistant looking over, your shoulder" (ALOYS)». «it is typically provided during the first learning task for which it is relevant. For subsequent [...] tasks, JIT information quickly fades away as the learners gain more expertise (a principle called fading).»
  - Unidades chicas: «JIT information is organized in small units, called information displays. Organization in small units is considered to be essential because only the presentation of relatively small amounts of new information at the same time can prevent processing overload.»
  - Práctica de partes: «it has been found to be effective only after ecposure [sic] to a simple version of the whole complex skill [...] One should thus identify the first task class for which performance of the routine aspect is required, and initiate part-task practice during this task class, preferably after learning tasks with ample learner support have already been worked on.»
- Frerejean, van Merriënboer, Kirschner, Roex, Aertgeerts y Marcellis (2019), *Designing instruction for complex learning: 4C/ID in higher education*, European Journal of Education 54(4), 513-524, doi 10.1111/ejed.12363. [L] de la versión condensada en LibreTexts (McDonald y West, *Design for Learning*), que dice: «This is a condensed version of Frerejean [...] (2019) [...] and is printed here under the same license as the original.» https://socialsci.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Education_and_Professional_Development/Design_for_Learning_-_Principles_Processes_and_Praxis_(McDonald_and_West)/02%3A_Instructional_Design_Knowledge/06%3A_Instructional_Design_Processes/6.03%3A_Designing_Instruction_For_Complex_Learning. El PDF de Wiley no se pudo bajar (página con JavaScript).

  - Andamiaje: «In a process of scaffolding, this support and guidance are gradually withdrawn until the learners are able to independently carry out tasks of a certain level of complexity before engaging in more complex tasks (i.e., the next task class).»
  - El caso: «The Amsterdam University of Applied Sciences in the Netherlands designed a course on Android app development using the 4C/ID model». «The teacher demonstrates the development of an application while thinking aloud and asks students to build an identical application on their computers. In subsequent tasks, students receive partially‐completed applications they must finish (i.e., completion tasks), while the final tasks in a task class are conventional tasks without any support and guidance.»
  - IT: «providing supportive and procedural information constituted a serious challenge, as both are subject to frequent and unpredictable changes [...] the course designers depended less on pre‐prepared lectures or recorded videos and instead referred students to a list of external sources, including manuals and Android developer documentation. Not only is it easier to keep a list of links up to date, it also creates a more authentic situation where students learn to study official documentation, as they would do in practice.»
- van Merriënboer, Clark y de Croock (2002), *Blueprints for complex learning: The 4C/ID-model*, ETR&D 50(2), 39-61, doi 10.1007/BF02504993 (volumen, número y páginas por Crossref). [M] resumen de ERIC, https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ652497: «focusing on learning tasks, supportive information, just-in-time information, and part-task practice». Springer cobra el PDF; las copias en elearn.uni-sofia.bg y willus.com son de autorización dudosa y no se usaron.

[M] resumen de ERIC, https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ737564: «this effect is augmented when both information types are presented just-in-time (i.e., declarative information "before" practice and procedural information "during" practice)». «Eighty-five students (49 male, 36 female; M = 15.2 years, SD = 0.59) participated in a 2 x 2 factorial experiment». «Transfer test scores and transfer efficiency scores support the first hypothesis; the second hypothesis was not supported.» (La primera hipótesis: «presenting this information piece-by-piece during practice [...] frees up working memory and facilitates learning».)

- Ostinelli (2024), *Testing Understanding by Design*, New Zealand Journal of Teachers' Work 21(1), 104-119, https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1431811.pdf. [L].
  - «While there are plenty of articles describing various experiences in classrooms and schools showing the benefits of UbD, rarely has this methodology been the object of experimental or quasi-experimental studies.»
