# Fuentes para los avisos de Didácticas específicas (issue #151)

Cinco avisos en cuatro notas. Se cerraron cuatro: los tres estudios de PCK, la didáctica de las ciencias, la de idiomas y la de trazado (Lopez et al. y Xie et al.). El de IA se achicó: Margulieux et al. (2024) y Bassner et al. (2025) se verificaron, Kazemitabaar et al. se aclaró (son dos trabajos), y Geng et al. (2025) y Xiao et al. (2026) no se pudieron identificar.

Los extractos en inglés son literales de lo que se leyó. Los metadatos (volumen, número, páginas, autores) salen de Crossref y OpenAlex por DOI. Las páginas de National Academies se leyeron con un lector que corta las citas en fragmentos; los fragmentos se citan tal cual.

## 1. Evidencia del PCK (`Didacticas-especificas/conocimiento-pedagogico-del-contenido.md`)

**Faltaba**: los resultados de Hill, Rowan y Ball (2005), Baumert et al. (2010) y Sadler et al. (2013). La nota solo tenía título, año y autores.

**Se encontró** (los tres, **abstract**; los textos completos están detrás de pago o, en el caso de Baumert, el repositorio de Regensburg devolvió 401):

- Hill, H. C., Rowan, B. y Ball, D. L. (2005), *Effects of Teachers' Mathematical Knowledge for Teaching on Student Achievement*, American Educational Research Journal 42(2):371-406. https://doi.org/10.3102/00028312042002371.
  - «The authors used a linear mixed-model methodology in which first and third graders' mathematical achievement gains over a year were nested within teachers, who in turn were nested within schools.»
  - «They found that teachers' mathematical knowledge was significantly related to student achievement gains in both first and third grades after controlling for key student- and teacher-level covariates.»
  - «[…] obtained via a measure focusing on the specialized mathematical knowledge and skills used in teaching mathematics.»
- Baumert, J., Kunter, M., Blum, W., Brunner, M., Voss, T., Jordan, A., Klusmann, U., Krauss, S., Neubrand, M. y Tsai, Y.-M. (2010), *Teachers' Mathematical Knowledge, Cognitive Activation in the Classroom, and Student Progress*, American Educational Research Journal 47(1):133-180. https://doi.org/10.3102/0002831209345157.
  - «It reports findings from a 1-year study conducted in Germany with a representative sample of Grade 10 classes and their mathematics teachers.»
  - «Teachers' pedagogical content knowledge was theoretically and empirically distinguishable from their content knowledge.»
  - «Multilevel structural equation models revealed a substantial positive effect of pedagogical content knowledge on students' learning gains that was mediated by the provision of cognitive activation and individual learning support.»
  - El nombre COACTIV no aparece en el abstract; es el nombre conocido del proyecto. Si se prefiere no usarlo sin fuente, se puede sacar de la tabla.
- Sadler, P. M., Sonnert, G., Coyle, H. P., Cook-Smith, N. y Miller, J. L. (2013), *The Influence of Teachers' Knowledge on Student Learning in Middle School Physical Science Classrooms*, American Educational Research Journal 50(5):1020-1049. https://doi.org/10.3102/0002831213477680.
  - «This study examines the relationship between teacher knowledge and student learning for 9,556 students of 181 middle school physical science teachers.»
  - «For items that had a very popular wrong answer, the teachers who could identify this misconception had larger classroom gains, much larger than if the teachers knew only the correct answer.»
  - «On items on which students did not exhibit misconceptions, teacher subject matter knowledge alone accounted for higher student gains.»
  - «[…] a teacher's ability to identify students' most common wrong answer on multiple-choice items, a form of pedagogical content knowledge […]».

**Qué se cambió**: una tabla con los tres estudios (quiénes y resultado según su resumen) en lugar del párrafo de «lo que se les atribuye»; las fuentes, con título, volumen y páginas. Corrección menor: la nota decía «física de secundaria» para Sadler et al.; el estudio es de *middle school physical science* (ciencias físicas de secundaria básica). El aviso se sacó.

**Sigue faltando**: los tres textos completos (tamaños de efecto). La nota ya dice que solo se leyó el resumen.

El aviso se sacó.

**Sigue faltando**: el texto de *Taking Science to School* más allá del resumen ejecutivo; el artículo de Hake entero; una fuente sobre indagación guiada en ciencias (la nota de [guía o descubrimiento](../Aprendizaje-activo-e-indagacion/guia-o-descubrimiento.md) cubre la disputa general).

## 3. Didáctica de idiomas para quien enseña (`Didacticas-especificas/didacticas-especificas.md`)

**Faltaba**: la didáctica de idiomas desde quien enseña.

**Se encontró**:

- Ellis, R. (diciembre de 2008), *Principles of Instructed Second Language Acquisition*, CAL Digest, Center for Applied Linguistics. https://cal.org/principlesofinstructedsecondlanguageacquisition (PDF). **Texto completo**.
  - «Second language acquisition (SLA) researchers do not agree how instruction can best facilitate language learning.»
  - «The principles described in this digest, therefore, are intended to provide teachers with a basis for argument and for reflection and not as a set of prescriptions or proscriptions about how to teach.»
  - Principios, tal cual: «Principle 1: Instruction needs to ensure that learners develop both a rich repertoire of formulaic expressions and a rule-based competence»; «Principle 2: Instruction needs to ensure that learners focus predominantly on meaning»; «Principle 3: Instruction needs to ensure that learners also focus on form»; «Principle 4: Instruction needs to focus on developing implicit knowledge of the second language while not neglecting explicit knowledge»; «Principle 5: Instruction needs to take into account the learner's built-in syllabus»; «Principle 6: Successful instructed language learning requires extensive second language input»; «Principle 7: Successful instructed language learning also requires opportunities for output»; «Principle 8: The opportunity to interact in the second language is central to developing second language proficiency»; «Principle 9: Instruction needs to take account of individual differences in learners»; «Principle 10: In assessing learners' second language proficiency, it is important to examine free as well as controlled production».
  - Es la versión divulgativa de Ellis (2005), *Principles of instructed language learning*, System 33(2):209-224, DOI 10.1016/j.system.2004.12.006, que no se pudo leer (ScienceDirect devolvió 403). El vínculo entre los dos es del buscador, no del digest.
- Norris, J. M. y Ortega, L. (2000), *Effectiveness of L2 Instruction: A Research Synthesis and Quantitative Meta-analysis*, Language Learning 50(3):417-528. https://doi.org/10.1111/0023-8333.00136. **Abstract** (ya citado en `Ingles/Adquisicion/instruccion-explicita.md`).
  - «Comparisons of average effect sizes from 49 unique sample studies reporting sufficient data indicated that focused L2 instruction results in large target-oriented gains, that explicit types of instruction are more effective than implicit types, and that Focus on Form and Focus on Forms interventions result in equivalent and large effects.»
- Bryfonski, L. y McKay, T. (2019), *TBLT implementation and evaluation: A meta-analysis*, Language Teaching Research 23(5):603-632 (en línea el 22/12/2017). https://doi.org/10.1177/1362168817744389. **Abstract**.
  - «no studies to date have synthesized the effects of long-term implementation of TBLT in authentic language classrooms»
  - «Findings based on a sample of 52 studies revealed an overall positive and strong effect ( d = 0.93) for TBLT implementation on a variety of learning outcomes.»
- Lightbown y Spada, *How Languages Are Learned* (5.ª ed., 2021), ya está en `Ingles/Fundamentos/bibliografia-de-ingles.md`; no se volvió a buscar. El MCER no se usó: el tema Inglés ya tiene `Ingles/Fundamentos/niveles-mcer.md`.

**Qué se cambió**: la viñeta de Idiomas apunta a una subsección nueva, «Idiomas: lo que decide quien enseña», con los principios de Ellis (resumidos, salvo el 9) y los dos metaanálisis. El aviso se sacó.

**Sigue faltando**: Ellis (2005) en *System*; algo específico de adultos en cursos profesionales.

## 4. Trazado y escritura de código (`Didacticas-especificas/ensenar-programacion.md`)

**Se encontró**:

- Lopez, M., Whalley, J., Robbins, P. y Lister, R. (2008), *Relationships between reading, tracing and writing skills in introductory programming*, ICER '08:101-112, DOI 10.1145/1404520.1404531. https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/10806/1/2008001530.pdf. **Texto completo** (repositorio de la UTS).
  - «The exam was undertaken by 78 students at the end of a first semester of programming in Java. Thirty eight of those students gave the institutionally required approval for their exam work to be used as research data.»

  - «A Pearson correlation analysis produced a correlation of 0.5586 between (Explain) and (Writing). […] The relationship accounts for 31% of the variability; adjusted R2 was 29%.»

  - «the co-variances identified in this study are not, alone, evidence of causality.»
- Xie, B., Nelson, G. L. y Ko, A. J. (2018), *An Explicit Strategy to Scaffold Novice Program Tracing*, SIGCSE '18:344-349, DOI 10.1145/3159450.3159527. https://faculty.washington.edu/ajko/papers/Xie2018TracingStrategies.pdf. **Texto completo** (sitio del autor).

**Qué se cambió**: dos párrafos nuevos, uno por estudio, con población, diseño y cifras; Lopez et al. aclarado como correlación. Las fuentes pasan de «solo metadatos» a texto completo. La frase de Margulieux et al. (2020) «el estudio de programación con resultado concreto mejor verificado de esta base» dejó de ser cierta y se cambió por «uno de los pocos estudios de programación de esta base con un resultado medido contra un grupo de comparación». **Sigue faltando**: Lister et al. (2004), que sigue con solo metadatos (no estaba en el aviso).

## 5. IA y programación (`Didacticas-especificas/ensenar-programacion-con-ia.md`)

**Faltaba**: un experimento con adultos, una revisión sistemática, y Margulieux et al. (ITiCSE 2024), Bassner et al. (2025), Geng et al. (2025) y Xiao et al. (2026). Además, aclarar la sede de Kazemitabaar et al. (2024).

**Se encontró**:

- Margulieux, L. E., Prather, J., Reeves, B. N., Becker, B. A., Cetin Uzun, G., Loksa, D., Leinonen, J. y Denny, P. (2024), *Self-Regulation, Self-Efficacy, and Fear of Failure Interactions with How Novices Use LLMs to Solve Programming Problems*, ITiCSE 2024 V. 1:276-282, DOI 10.1145/3649217.3653621. https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10580378. **Abstract y sección de método**.
  - «In this repeated-measures, mixed-methods research, we examined students' patterns of using generative AI with qualitative student reflections and their self-regulation, self-efficacy, and fear of failure with quantitative instruments at multiple times throughout the semester.»
  - «students with higher self-efficacy, lower fear of failure, or higher prior grades tended to use AI less or later in the problem-solving process and rated it as less useful than others.»
  - «we found no relationship between students' self-regulation strategies and their use of AI.»
  - «Students who used AI less or later in problem-solving also had higher grades in the course, but this is most likely due to prior characteristics as our data do not suggest that this is a causal relationship.»
  - Método: «Data were collected from students in an introductory programming course at Towson University, a mid-size, public US university, that ran from January to May 2023. Students were encouraged to use GenAI tools to support their learning and problem-solving as they wished.» «In total, 54 students participated in the study.» «the final quantitative dataset included 40 participants».
- Bassner, P., Lenk-Ostendorf, B., Beinstingel, R., Wasner, T. y Krusche, S., *Less stress, better scores, same learning: The dissociation of performance and learning in AI-supported programming education*, Computers and Education: Artificial Intelligence 10, art. 100537, DOI 10.1016/j.caeai.2025.100537. **Abstract**. Fecha: OpenAlex lo da como 2025; Crossref fecha el volumen en junio de 2026. La nota mantiene «2025».
  - «A three-arm randomized controlled trial was conducted in an introductory programming (CS1) course at TUM (N=275).»
  - «Participants completed a 90-minute exercise on concurrency […] in one of three conditions: (1) Iris, a scaffolded tutor providing calibrated hints while withholding full solutions; (2) ChatGPT, unrestricted assistance that can provide complete solutions; (3) no-AI control using traditional web resources.»
  - «Both AI groups achieved substantially higher exercise scores than the control group».
  - «Despite these performance gains, neither AI condition produced greater pre–post knowledge gains or code-comprehension advantages.»
  - «Only Iris increased intrinsic motivation.»
  - «In this setting, generative AI acted primarily as a performance aid rather than a learning enhancer.»
  - **Corrección para otra nota**: `Tecnologia-educativa/.investigacion/ia-practica-adaptativa-plataformas.md` le da el DOI 10.1016/j.caeai.2025.100509; el correcto, según Crossref, es 10.1016/j.caeai.2025.100537.

  - Kazemitabaar, M., Ye, R., Wang, X., Henley, A. Z., Denny, P., Craig, M. y Grossman, T. (2024), *CodeAid: Evaluating a Classroom Deployment of an LLM-based Programming Assistant that Balances Student and Educator Needs*, CHI '24, pp. 1-20, DOI 10.1145/3613904.3642773 (arXiv 2401.11314). **Abstract**: «We developed CodeAid, an LLM-powered programming assistant delivering helpful, technically correct responses, without revealing code solutions.» «We deployed CodeAid in a programming class of 700 students for a 12-week semester. ## Cotejo posterior

Ajustes tras un cotejo de cada afirmación contra los extractos:

- **CodeAid, «cuatro criterios de diseño»**: confirmado en el abstract: «Our findings reveal four design considerations for future educational AI assistants: D1) exploiting AI's unique benefits; D2) simplifying query formulation while promoting cognitive engagement; D3) avoiding direct responses while encouraging motivated learning; and D4) maintaining transparency and control for students to asses and steer AI responses.»
- **Margulieux et al., «40 con datos completos»**: confirmado en el método: «Of the 40 participants with complete datasets, 6 were missing demographic data.» «Universidad pública de EE. UU.»: «Towson University, a mid-size, public US university».
- **Margulieux et al. y Prather et al.**: se sacó «ven el mismo reparto desde el otro lado». Ahora la nota dice que los dos repartos son distintos (en Prather et al. quien sabía usaba la IA con provecho; en Margulieux et al., la usaba menos) y que conciliarlos es lectura de esta base. La causa pasa a la cita literal «most likely due to prior characteristics».
- **Kazemitabaar**: se sacó de la nota el párrafo sobre lo que decía antes; la historia queda en este informe.
- **Lopez et al., «in plain English»**: confirmado en el abstract: «A correlation was also found between performance on "explain in plain English" tasks and code writing.»
- **Xie et al., «los 8 … que informaron su nota»**: confirmado: «Of the 24 participants, 17 responded (8 control, 9 strategy, with 1 not sharing their grade).» «Seven of the 8 strategy group participants who provided midterm grades said they used the strategy on the midterm. These 8 scored on average 7% better than the control group».
- **PCK**: se sacaron «EE. UU.» (Hill y Sadler) y «COACTIV», que no tienen extracto; «muestras escolares grandes» pasa a «alumnos de escuela»; «la segunda mitad de la definición de Shulman, medida» queda marcada como lectura de esta base.
- **Ciencias**: «dos ideas verificadas» pasa a «dos ideas con fuente» y aclara que se leyeron solo resúmenes. NRC: «de EE. UU.» pasa a «grados K-8, según su título». Sadler et al. «miden algo afín», y la conexión queda marcada como de esta base. Hake: «no un experimento con asignación al azar» pasa a «una encuesta ("survey") de cursos ya existentes; el resumen no menciona asignación al azar».
- **Idiomas**: se sacó «Dos metaanálisis respaldan decisiones de esa lista». Norris y Ortega: el resumen dice «experimental and quasi-experimental investigations»; su relación con el principio 3 queda como lectura de esta base. Bryfonski y McKay se conectan con el principio 2, donde Ellis escribe: «A task-based approach to language teaching is perhaps the best way of achieving this.» El resumen de Bryfonski y McKay habla de «authentic language classrooms». «Los dos son de aulas de idiomas en general» pasa a «Ninguno de los dos resúmenes habla de adultos en cursos profesionales».
