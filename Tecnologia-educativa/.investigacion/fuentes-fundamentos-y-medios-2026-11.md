# Fuentes para Fundamentos y Medios y aprendizaje (issue #154)

Seis avisos en cinco notas. Se cerraron tres: la analogía del camión de Clark, *Failure to Disrupt* (con reseñas, no con el libro) y las ediciones posteriores de la OCDE y la UNESCO. Se achicaron tres: la historia anterior a 1970 en las dos notas de Fundamentos (falta *Teachers and Machines* entero) y el estado del debate de los medios (falta una revisión fuera de la realidad virtual).

Los extractos en inglés son literales de lo que se leyó. Kozma (1994) y Skinner (1958) son PDF escaneados: el texto sale del OCR, con algún error de letra que se corrigió solo donde era evidente.

## 1. Historia anterior a 1970 (`Fundamentos/fundamentos-de-tecnologia-educativa.md`)

**Faltaba**: la historia anterior a 1970 (radio, cine, televisión educativa, máquinas de enseñar) y una ruta de aprendizaje con ejercicios. La ruta no se buscó, por encargo.

**Se encontró**: las mismas dos fuentes del aviso 2 (ver ahí los extractos).

- Cuban, L. (1986), *Teachers and Machines: The Classroom Use of Technology since 1920*, Teachers College Press. Selección de citas con páginas, «Blumberg's Selected Quotations from Cuban 1986», para el curso CS92 de la Universidad Brown: https://cs.brown.edu/courses/cs092/2004/cs92.cuban86.html. **Secundaria** (citas textuales del libro, no el libro). El libro está en el Internet Archive (`teachersmachines0000cuba`, «x, 134 p.», ISBN 080772792X) pero con acceso restringido; no se pudo leer.
- Skinner, B. F. (1958), *Teaching Machines*, Science 128(3330):969-977. **Texto completo**, copia de JSTOR en Nova Southeastern University: https://app.nova.edu/toolbox/instructionalproducts/edd8124/fall11/1958-Skinner-TeachingMachines.pdf.

Extractos que sostienen la oración nueva:

- Edison (1922), citado por Cuban (p. 9): «I believe that the motion picture is destined to revolutionize our educational system and that in a few years it will supplant largely, if not entirely, the use of textbooks.»
- Radio (p. 24): un estudio de seis años «concluded in 1943 that "radio has not been accepted as a full-fledged member of the educational family."»
- Televisión (p. 38): «There was not substantial difference between the amount of information learned from televised lessons and information conveyed through conventional instructional approaches used by the classroom teacher -- again, as measured by standardized achievement tests.»
- Skinner (sección sobre la máquina; la página no se anotó): «This may suggest mass production, but the effect upon each student is surprisingly like that of a private tutor.»

**Qué se cambió**: una oración y media en «Leer la tecnología educativa con su historia» (Edison, radio, televisión, Skinner); Cuban 1986 y Skinner 1958 en `fuentes`. El aviso se achicó.

**Sigue faltando**: *Teachers and Machines* entero y la ruta de aprendizaje.

## 2. El ciclo anterior a las computadoras y las máquinas de Skinner (`Fundamentos/promesas-incumplidas-de-la-tecnologia-educativa.md`)

**Sigue faltando**: el libro de Cuban entero, y los estudios de radio y televisión que Cuban cita (la tabla los da según Cuban). En el OCR de Skinner, el año de la queja de Pressey sale «1972», que es imposible en un artículo de 1958 (probablemente 1932); no se usó.

## 4. La analogía del camión de Clark (`Medios-y-aprendizaje/debate-de-los-medios.md`)

**Faltaba**: la cita literal del camión de reparto; los artículos de 1994 no se habían abierto.

**Se encontró**:

- Kozma, R. B. (1994), *Will media influence learning? Reframing the debate*, ETR&D 42(2):7-19 («ETR&D, Vol. 42, No. 2, 1994, pp. 7-19 ISSN 1042-1629»). **Texto completo**: el escaneo de Springer en una carpeta de curso de la Universidad de Kentucky, con nombre de archivo engañoso: https://www.uky.edu/~gmswan3/609/Kozma_1991.pdf. Extractos:
  - La cita de Clark (1983): «Rather, " . . . media are mere vehicles that deliver instruction but do not influence student achievement any more than the truck that delivers our groceries causes changes in our nutrition" (p. 445).» Antes: Clark «claimed that they provide consistent evidence " . . . for the generalization that there are no learning benefits to be gained from employing any specific medium to deliver instruction" (p. 445)» y «Clark concludes that " . . . media do not influence learning under any conditions" (p. 445)».
  - «Perhaps the appropriate question is not do but will media influence learning. Educational technology is a design science (Simon, 1981, Glaser, 1976), not a natural science.» «If there is no relationship between media and learning it may be because we have not yet made one.»
  - Sobre el camión: «The theoretical frame of reference implicit in these studies--that of presentation and response---is aptly characterized by Clark's delivery truck metaphor: The medium is an inert conveyer of an active stimulus to which the learner makes a behavioral response.»
  - Resumen de los dos entornos: «the learners in the ThinkerTools project benefited from the use of computers because the capabilities of this medium were employed to provide representations and perform or model operations that were salient to the task and that the learners had difficulty providing for themselves. Learners in the Jasper project benefited from the use of television because the capability of the medium was used to present problems embedded in complex social contexts […]» «when learners have difficulty providing representations and operations that are sufficient for learning, either because of limited prior knowledge, limitations in working memory, or other reasons, they will likely benefit from the use of the capability of a particular medium».
  - Conclusión: «if we move from "Do media influence learning?" to "In what ways can we use the capabilities of media to influence learning for particular students, tasks, and situations?" we will both advance the development of our field and contribute to the restructuring of schools and the improvement of education and training.»
- Clark, R. E. (1983), *Reconsidering Research on Learning from Media*, Review of Educational Research 53(4):445-459. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ294173. **Abstract de ERIC**: «Consistent evidence is found for the generalization that there are no learning benefits to be gained from employing any specific medium to deliver instruction.»
- Clark, R. E. (1994), *Media Will Never Influence Learning*, ETR&D 42(2):21-29. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ488324. **Abstract de ERIC**: «Discusses different points of view about the impact of media on learning, motivation, and efficiency gains from instruction. The author's arguments about research and theory of media effects are summarized; reactions to the claim that media do not influence learning are characterized; and responses are given to Robert Kozma's specific criticisms». Crossref: junio de 1994, 42(2), 21-29. OpenAlex: cerrado, sin copia en repositorios. El PDF de Springer pide autenticación.
- Hastings, N. B. y Tracey, M. W. (2005), *Does Media Affect Learning: Where Are We Now?*, TechTrends. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ684131. **Abstract**: repite la cita del camión con «(p. 445)» y concluye que la revisión de los argumentos «indicates there is, and always has been, significantly more agreement on this subject than the debate would indicate.»
- Lawson et al. (2024), ver aviso 5; cita también Clark (1983, p. 448): «It was Mielke (1968) who reminded us that when examining the effects of different media, only the media being compared can be different. All other aspects of the treatments, including the subject matter content and method of instruction, must be identical».

**Qué se cambió**: el aviso del camión se reemplazó por la cita literal, atribuida a Clark (1983, p. 445) según Kozma. Se sumó el resumen de Clark 1983. La sección de Kozma pasó de «el resumen no detalla su argumento» a su argumento con citas. El aviso de arriba ahora dice que Kozma se leyó entero y Clark no. El bloque `[!IMPORTANT]` dice ahora que ninguna revisión encontrada declara ganador (Lawson et al., Hastings y Tracey). `fuentes`: Clark 1983, Clark 1994 (ERIC), Kozma (texto completo), Lawson, Hastings y Tracey.

**Sigue faltando**: Clark (1983) y Clark (1994) enteros. La frase de Clark 1994 «is neither sufficient for nor necessary to learning» sigue sin cotejar. Las réplicas del mismo número (Jonassen et al., Morrison, Reiser, Shrock) no se leyeron; del de Morrison (1994) hay abstract en ERIC (EJ488326): «Clark and Kozma are raising different questions». Lawson et al. listan también una segunda ronda en ETR&D 42(3): Clark (1994b), *Media and method*, y Kozma (1994a), *A reply: Media and methods*, 42(3):11-14.

## 5. Estado actual del debate (`Medios-y-aprendizaje/medios-y-aprendizaje.md`)

**Faltaba**: una revisión reciente que diga si el debate está resuelto o si domina «el método, no el medio».

**Se encontró**:

- Lawson, A. P., Marchand Martella, A., LaBonte, K., Delgado, C. Y., Zhao, F., Gluck, J. A., Munns, M. E., LeRoy, A. W. y Mayer, R. E. (2024), *Confounded or Controlled? A Systematic Review of Media Comparison Studies Involving Immersive Virtual Reality for STEM Education*, Educational Psychology Review 36:69, doi 10.1007/s10648-024-09908-8. **Texto completo**, copia de la Universidad de California: https://escholarship.org/uc/item/8zv0283x. Extractos:
  - «Our goal is not to contribute to the long-standing debate on the merits of media comparison research but rather is to pinpoint ways to improve the methodological rigor of this literature base.»
  - «Revisiting the media comparison debate, there are some, like Clark (1994a, 1994b), who argue that media do not influence learning and are mere delivery trucks whereas there are others, like Kozma (1994a, 1994b), who argue that media have unique attributes […] Regardless of the side of the debate a researcher is on—or if they fall somewhere in the middle—if we are to determine whether media influence learning, one or more instructional components should be selectively contrasted and studied in a systematic fashion to determine which components are most important for learning».
  - Abstract: «Thirty-eight published journal articles, conference proceedings, and dissertations related to IVR comparison studies in STEM education between the years 2013 and 2022 were coded according to 15 categories. […] Results indicated only 26% of all comparisons examined between an IVR and conventional condition were fully controlled on five key control criteria. Moreover, 40% of the comparisons had at least one confound related to instructional method and content. When looking at the outcomes of the studies, it was difficult to gather a clear picture of the benefits or pitfalls of IVR when much of the literature was confounded and/or lacked sufficient information».
  - De otros: «Honebein and Reigeluth (2021) reviewed 39 media comparison articles (years 1980–2019) in the journal ETRD and 41 media comparison articles (years 2009–2018) in other journals and found that the majority of comparative articles confounded the instructional media and the instructional methods.» (No se usó en la nota; Honebein y Reigeluth no se leyó.)
  - Discrepancia interna: el abstract dice 40 % con al menos una confusión; la conclusión dice «almost half of all comparisons had at least one confound». La nota usa el 40 % del abstract.
- Otras revisiones vistas por su abstract en ERIC, no usadas en la nota: Sickel (2019), *The Great Media Debate and TPACK*, Journal of Research on Technology in Education (EJ1217978), que «modernizing the media influence debate» y lo une con TPACK; Warnick y Burbules (2007), *Media Comparison Studies: Problems and Possibilities*, Teachers College Record (EJ820500): «no global statements can be made about such things».

**Qué se cambió**: una sección nueva, «Dónde está el debate hoy», con Lawson et al.; la frase «el debate sigue abierto en lo teórico y lo que quedó firme es la exigencia de método de Clark» va marcada como lectura de esta base. El aviso se achicó. `fuentes`: Clark 1994 pasa a ERIC, Kozma a texto completo, Lawson nuevo.

**Sigue faltando**: una revisión general (no de un solo medio) que diga qué lectura domina. La idea de que «domina el método, no el medio» sigue sin una fuente que la declare así.

## 6. OCDE y UNESCO posteriores a 2015 (`Medios-y-aprendizaje/acceso-y-uso-de-la-tecnologia.md`)

**Faltaba**: ediciones posteriores de la OCDE y el informe GEM 2023 de la UNESCO.

**Se encontró**: las páginas de la OCDE dan 403 o piden JavaScript; se leyeron en el Internet Archive.

- OECD (2023), *PISA 2022 Results (Volume II): Learning During – and From – Disruption*, cap. 5, «Investments in a solid foundation for learning and well-being». https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-2022-results-volume-ii_a97db61c-en/full-report/component-12.html, leído en http://web.archive.org/web/20261013083854/https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-2022-results-volume-ii_a97db61c-en/full-report/component-12.html. **Texto del capítulo** (sin los gráficos). Extractos:
  - «Students who spent up to one hour per day on digital devices for learning activities in school (31% of students) scored 25 points higher in mathematics than students who spent no time, on average across OECD countries. Even after accounting for students' and schools' socio-economic profile, students scored 14 points higher».
  - «students who spent between 5 and 7 hours per day on digital devices for learning activities in school (7.8% of students) scored 12 points lower than students who spent between 3 and 5 hours per day; after accounting for students' and schools' socio-economic profile, the former group of students scored 10 points lower.»
  - Ocio en la escuela: «Students who spent up to one hour per day on digital devices for leisure activities (31% of students) scored 20 points higher in mathematics than students who spent no time. […] But students who spent more than an hour on digital devices for leisure scored lower in mathematics.»

  - Sobre causalidad, en el mismo capítulo (a propósito del tiempo de aprendizaje): «Given the cross-sectional nature of the PISA surveys […] PISA cannot determine causality.»
- OECD Directorate for Education and Skills (2024), *Students, digital devices and success*, «© OECD 2024», «Findings from PISA 2022 results». https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/students-digital-devices-and-success_9e4c0624-en.html; PDF leído en http://web.archive.org/web/20240627234300/https://www.oecd.org/pisa/aboutpisa/English%20V3_WEB.pdf. **Texto completo** (18 págs.). Extractos:
  - «the amount of time that children use smartphones and other digital devices for leisure at school is strongly negatively related to learning outcomes.»
  - «On average across OECD countries, students who spent up to one hour a day for leisure activities on digital devices at school scored 49 points higher in maths than their counterparts who spent five to seven hours daily glued to their screens, even after adjusting for students' and schools' socio-economic profiles.»
  - «59% of students across the OECD said their attention was diverted due to other students using phones, tablets or laptops in at least some maths lessons.»
  - «While the data suggest a positive relationship between the intentional integration of technology in school education and student performance, the use of smartphones and other digital devices for leisure can be a learning distraction.»

El informe completo no se leyó. Extractos de «Key messages»:

**¿Cambia lo dicho con el dato de 2015?** No. La OCDE 2015 (vía EdWeek) decía que hay poca evidencia de que más uso dé mejores puntajes; PISA 2022 muestra asociación positiva con el uso moderado para aprender y negativa con el uso intenso y con el ocio en la escuela, sin afirmar causa. La UNESCO 2023 dice lo mismo que la OCDE 2015 en el fondo. No hizo falta `[!IMPORTANT]` ni `[!WARNING]`. La oración de la nota que viene «de un resumen del buscador, sin verificar en el informe» (uso moderado mejor que raro, muy frecuente peor, con datos de 2012) queda como estaba: el patrón de 2022 se le parece, pero no verifica el dato de 2015.

El aviso se cerró. De paso, la reseña de Cuban (2001) en el *Christian Science Monitor* se leyó entera (ver abajo) y se actualizó su entrada en `fuentes`.

**Sigue faltando**: el informe *Students, Computers and Learning* (2015) sigue sin abrirse; PISA 2022 Volume V no se buscó; el informe GEM completo no se leyó.

## Fuera del tema

- `Ciencias-del-aprendizaje/Fundamentos/teorias-del-aprendizaje.md` (línea 57) dice que Pressey hizo máquinas «en 1927». Skinner (1958) dice «in the 1920's» y cita a Pressey en *School and Society* 23, 586 (1926). No es una contradicción, pero 1926 tiene fuente. Skinner (1958) leído entero puede reemplazar a «Skinner (1954) […] (según resumen)» para la idea de la máquina; no trae resultados medidos, así que no cierra el aviso de esa nota (línea 60).
- `Tecnologia-educativa/tecnologia-educativa.md` (línea 7) y otras notas del tema que citan Clark (1994) con «resumen del buscador» podrían pasar al abstract de ERIC (EJ488324) y sumar Kozma (1994) como texto completo. Son notas de otros.
- `Tecnologia-educativa/Fundamentos/mitos-de-tecnologia-educativa.md` (línea 33) y `Tecnologia-educativa/tecnologia-educativa.md` (línea 52) repiten el dato de la OCDE 2015. No los contradice nada de lo encontrado; podrían sumar PISA 2022 si se quiere el dato más nuevo.
