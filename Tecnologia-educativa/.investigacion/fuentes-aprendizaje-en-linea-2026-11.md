# Fuentes para los avisos de Aprendizaje en línea (issue #154)

Cinco avisos en cuatro notas. Se cerró uno: la tipología de Kizilcec, Piech y Schneider y el trabajo de Reich (2014). Se achicaron cuatro: qué reduce el abandono, la pandemia, los metaanálisis posteriores a 2013 y la evidencia experimental sobre video. No hubo contradicciones con lo que las notas ya decían.

Los extractos en inglés son literales de lo que se leyó. Las búsquedas en PubMed y PMC se hicieron con E-utilities; ERIC, con su API.

## 1. Qué reduce el abandono (`Aprendizaje-en-linea/aprendizaje-en-linea.md`)

**Faltaba**: evidencia experimental sobre qué reduce el abandono en un curso en línea.

**Se encontró**: Kizilcec, R. F., Reich, J., Yeomans, M., Dann, C., Brunskill, E., Lopez, G., Turkay, S., Williams, J. J. y Tingley, D. (2020), *Scaling up behavioral science interventions in online education*, PNAS 117(26):14900-14905, doi 10.1073/pnas.1921417117. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7334459 (PMID 32541050). **Texto completo** (XML de PMC por E-utilities).

- Abstract: «we test a set of established interventions over 2.5 y, with one-quarter million students, from nearly every country, across 247 online courses offered by Harvard, the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, and Stanford. We hypothesized that the interventions would produce medium-to-large effects as in prior studies, but this is not supported by our results.»
- «269,169 students across 247 courses».
- «Self-regulation interventions raised student engagement in the first few weeks but not final completion rates.»
- «The “plan-making” interventions prompted students to concretely describe when and how they will complete required coursework for the entire course.»
- «the effects of our plan-making interventions attenuated after 1 to 2 wk, and were not detectable in the final course completion rates.»
- «The “value-relevance” intervention is a motivational activity that asks students to indicate important values and write about how taking the course reflects and reinforces what is most important to them.»
- «Value-relevance interventions raised completion rates in developing countries to close the global achievement gap, but only in courses with a global gap.»
- «In courses with a significant global gap, the value-relevance intervention increased the average completion rate among students in less-developed countries by 2.79 pp in the first year (95% CI = [1.30, 4.27], t = 3.68, P < 0.001) and by 2.74 pp in the second year».
- «In courses without a global gap (or where it was reversed), post hoc analyses indicate that the intervention lowered the average completion rate among students in less-developed countries (year 1: β = −1.62 pp».
- «Scaling behavioral science interventions across various online learning contexts can reduce their average effectiveness by an order-of-magnitude.»
- «the question of “what works?” would be replaced with “what works, for whom, right here?”»
- Dato de contexto, no usado en la nota: «Course completion rates are often low: Around 20% in Harvard University, the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT), and Stanford University massive open online courses (MOOCs) among students who intend to complete».

**Qué cambió**: tres párrafos y una lista con el experimento. El aviso se achicó.

**Sigue faltando**: evidencia experimental sobre cohortes, plazos fijos, comunidad y contacto con un docente.

## 2. La pandemia (`Aprendizaje-en-linea/aprendizaje-en-linea.md`)

**Faltaba**: Betthäuser, Bach-Mortensen y Engzell (2023); Hanushek y Woessmann (2020); UNESCO.

**Se encontró**:

- Betthäuser, B. A., Bach-Mortensen, A. M. y Engzell, P. (2023), *A systematic review and meta-analysis of the evidence on learning during the COVID-19 pandemic*, Nature Human Behaviour 7(3):375-385, doi 10.1038/s41562-022-01506-4. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36717609/. **Abstract** (la página de Nature pide autenticación):
  - «we conduct a pre-registered systematic review, quality appraisal and meta-analysis of 42 studies across 15 countries to assess the magnitude of learning deficits during the pandemic. We find a substantial overall learning deficit (Cohen's d = -0.14, 95% confidence interval -0.17 to -0.10), which arose early in the pandemic and persists over time.»
  - «Learning deficits are particularly large among children from low socio-economic backgrounds. They are also larger in maths than in reading and in middle-income countries relative to high-income countries. There is a lack of evidence on learning progress during the pandemic in low-income countries.»
- Hanushek, E. y Woessmann, L. (2020), *The Economic Impacts of Learning Losses*, OECD Education Working Paper 225. https://hanushek.stanford.edu/sites/default/files/publications/Hanushek%2BWoessmann%202020%20OECD%20Education%20Working%20Paper%20No.%20225.pdf. **Texto completo** (copia del autor):
  - «While the precise learning losses are not yet known, existing research suggests that the students in grades 1-12 affected by the closures might expect some 3 percent lower income over their entire lifetimes. For nations, the lower long-term growth related to such losses might yield an average of 1.5 percent lower annual GDP for the remainder of the century.»
  - «The economic losses will be more deeply felt by disadvantaged students.»
  - «Looking at the losses associated with one-third of a year closure, the pooled estimates indicate that current students will suffer 2.6% loss in income across their entire career.»
- UNESCO: no se pudo leer. La página de respuesta educativa de UNESCO cortó la conexión y el informe de políticas de la ONU (agosto de 2020) no bajó como PDF. El buscador repite la cifra de 1.600 millones de alumnos sin escuela en abril de 2020 citando al Banco Mundial y a la ONU; no se usó.

**Qué cambió**: dos párrafos, uno por fuente. El de Hanushek y Woessmann aclara que es una proyección, no una medición. El aviso se achicó.

**Sigue faltando**: los datos de UNESCO sobre alumnos y duración de los cierres, y el texto completo de Betthäuser et al.

## 3. Metaanálisis posteriores a 2013 (`Aprendizaje-en-linea/en-linea-y-presencial.md`)

**Faltaba**: metaanálisis posteriores a 2013 sobre en línea frente a presencial.

**Se encontró**:

- Bernard, R. M., Borokhovski, E., Schmid, R. F., Tamim, R. M. y Abrami, P. C. (2014), *A meta-analysis of blended learning and technology use in higher education: from the general to the applied*, Journal of Computing in Higher Education 26(1):87-122, doi 10.1007/s12528-013-9077-3 (volumen y páginas, de Crossref). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1040524. **Abstract de ERIC** (Springer pide autenticación):
  - «a meta-analysis of a sub-collection of comparative studies of BL and classroom instruction (CI) from a larger systematic review of technology integration».
  - «The results indicate that, in terms of achievement outcomes, BL conditions exceed CI conditions by about one-third of a standard deviation (g+ = 0.334, k = 117, p < 0.001) and that the kind of computer support used (i.e., cognitive support vs. content/presentational support) and the presence of one or more interaction treatments (e.g., student-student/-teacher/-content interaction) serve to enhance student achievement.»
- Pei, L. y Wu, H. (2019), *Does online learning work better than offline learning in undergraduate medical education? A systematic review and meta-analysis*, Medical Education Online, doi 10.1080/10872981.2019.1666538. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31526248/. **Abstract** (vía OpenAlex):
  - «Five databases and four key journals of medical education were searched using 10 terms and their Boolean combinations during 2000-2017.»
  - «16 out of 3,700 published articles were identified.»
  - «The meta-analyses affirmed a statistically significant difference between online and offline learning for knowledge and skill outcomes based on post-test scores (SMD = 0.81; 95% CI: 0.43, 1.20; p < 0.0001; n = 15).»
  - «The meta-analyses discovered no significant difference when using pre- and post-test score gains (SMD = 3.03; 95% CI: -0.13, 4.13; p = 0.07; n = 3).»

**Qué cambió**: una lista con los dos metaanálisis y un párrafo que los pesa como inferencia de esta base («cuatro veces» es 0,81 / 0,20). El aviso se achicó.

**Sigue faltando**: un metaanálisis posterior a 2013 de cursos totalmente en línea, fuera de medicina, leído entero. Nota para quien revise: el intervalo de Pei y Wu para la ganancia (−0,13 a 4,13 con SMD = 3,03) es raro tal como lo da el abstract; no se usó esa cifra.

## 4. Kizilcec, Piech y Schneider y Reich (`Aprendizaje-en-linea/finalizacion-de-mooc.md`)

**Faltaba**: la tipología de 2013 y el trabajo de Reich (2014) sobre intención y retención.

**Se encontró**:

- Kizilcec, R. F., Piech, C. y Schneider, E. (2013), *Deconstructing Disengagement: Analyzing Learner Subpopulations in Massive Open Online Courses*, LAK 2013:170-179, doi 10.1145/2460296.2460330. https://web.stanford.edu/~cpiech/bio/papers/deconstructingDisengagement.pdf. **Texto completo**.
  - «As MOOCs grow in popularity, the relatively low completion rates of learners has been a central criticism. This focus on completion rates, however, reflects a monolithic view of disengagement».
  - «In an analysis of three computer science MOOCs, the classifier consistently identifies four prototypical trajectories of engagement. The most notable of these is the learners who stay engaged through the course without taking assessments.»
  - Cursos: «“Computer Science 101” covers high school level content (HS-level), “Algorithms: Design and Analysis” covers undergraduate level content (UG-level), and “Probabilistic Graphical Models” is a graduate level course (GS-level)». Tabla 1, «Active Learners»: 46096, 26887, 21108.
  - «‘Completing’: learners who completed the majority of the assessments offered in the class.»
  - «‘Auditing’: learners who did assessments infrequently if at all and engaged instead by watching video lectures. Students in this cluster followed the course for the majority of its duration. No students in this cluster obtained course credit.»
  - «‘Disengaging’: learners who did assessments at the beginning of the course but then have a marked decrease in engagement […] The moments at which the learners disengage differ, but it is generally in the first third of the class.»
  - «‘Sampling’: learners who watched video lectures for only one or two assessment periods».
  - Tabla 2 (leída con `pdftotext -layout`): «Course Auditing Completing Disengaging Sampling / HS 6% 27% 28% 39% / UG 6% 8% 12% 74% / GS 9% 5% 6% 80%».
- Reich, J. (lunes 8 de diciembre de 2014), *MOOC Completion and Retention in the Context of Student Intent*, EDUCAUSE Review. https://er.educause.edu/articles/2014/12/mooc-completion-and-retention-in-the-context-of-student-intent. El sitio devuelve 403; se leyó el **texto completo** en Internet Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/2015/http://www.educause.edu/ero/article/mooc-completion-and-retention-context-student-intent.
  - «I draw on data from nine HarvardX courses, which had a total of 290,606 registrants and 79,525 survey responses.»
  - «Across nine HarvardX courses, 22 percent of students who intended to complete a course went on to earn a certificate.»
  - «On average across courses, 6 percent of intended-browsers, 7.5 percent of intended-auditors, and 10 percent of students with unsure commitments to the course earned a certificate.»
  - «For intended browsers, the median lifetime is 10 percent of the course, while for intended completers it is 35 percent of the course.»
  - «Nearly 10 percent of intended completers leave in a course's first few days.»
  - Dos cifras para lo mismo: «Of those who completed the survey, 63 percent indicated that they intended to earn a certificate; of those students, 24 percent did so.» y «Of the HarvardX students who responded to the pre-course surveys, 56 percent reported that they intended to complete their course. An average of 22 percent of these intend-completers across courses went on to actually earn a certificate.»
  - Período: «offered between September 2013 and June 2014».

**Qué cambió**: un párrafo y una tabla con las cuatro trayectorias, un párrafo con Reich y otro con la discrepancia 63/24 frente a 56/22. La interpretación de que 22 % es promedio por curso sale del texto («An average … across courses»); que 24 % sea sobre el total es inferencia y la nota lo dice. En «Qué cambia para quien arma un curso abierto» se sumó el 22 % de Reich y se reemplazó «no tiene evidencia verificada» por una referencia al experimento de Kizilcec et al. (2020). Aviso cerrado.

**Sigue faltando**: nada del aviso.

## 5. Evidencia experimental sobre video (`Aprendizaje-en-linea/video-educativo.md`)

**Faltaba**: evidencia experimental sobre aprendizaje con video, como las preguntas incrustadas.

**Se encontró**:

- Noetel, M. et al. (2021), *Video Improves Learning in Higher Education: A Systematic Review*, Review of Educational Research. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1290063. **Abstract de ERIC**:
  - «We searched five databases using 27 keywords to find randomized trials that measured the learning effects of video among college students.»
  - «After screening 329 full texts, 105 met inclusion criteria, with a pooled sample of 7,776 students. Swapping video for existing teaching methods led to small improvements in student learning (g = 0.28). Adding video to existing teaching led to strong learning benefits (g = 0.80). Although results may be subject to some experimental and publication biases, they suggest that videos are unlikely to be detrimental and usually improve student learning.»
- Szpunar, K. K., Khan, N. Y. y Schacter, D. L. (2013), *Interpolated memory tests reduce mind wandering and improve learning of online lectures*, PNAS 110(16):6313-6317, doi 10.1073/pnas.1221764110. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23576743/. **Abstract** (PMC pidió verificación de navegador y Europe PMC no devolvió el texto):
  - «In two experiments, we demonstrate that the simple act of interpolating online lectures with memory tests can help students sustain attention to lecture content in a manner that discourages task-irrelevant mind wandering activities, encourages task-relevant note-taking activities, and improves learning. Importantly, frequent testing was associated with reduced anxiety toward a final cumulative test and also with reductions in subjective estimates of cognitive demand.»

**Qué cambió**: sección nueva «Lo que muestran los experimentos». El aviso se achicó y conserva los enlaces a Mayer y carga cognitiva.

**Sigue faltando**: el texto completo de los dos, con muestras y efectos por tipo de video. No se registró el volumen y las páginas de Noetel et al.; Crossref devolvió primero el preprint de PsyArXiv (doi 10.31234/osf.io/kynez).

## Para revisar

- La discrepancia interna de Reich (2014): 63 % y 24 % frente a 56 % y 22 %.
- El efecto de Pei y Wu (SMD = 0,81) es mucho mayor que el de Means; la nota lo marca y no le da peso.
- Betthäuser et al., Pei y Wu, Bernard et al., Noetel et al. y Szpunar et al. se leyeron solo por su resumen; las notas lo dicen.

## Agregado después (cotejo)

El cotejo contra los extractos de este informe pidió acotar cinco frases, sin datos nuevos: Pei y Wu («16 estudios, buscados entre 2000 y 2017», porque el resumen da el período de búsqueda: «during 2000-2017»); quienes auditan siguen el curso «durante la mayor parte de su duración» («for the majority of its duration»); Betthäuser, «de los países de ingreso bajo falta evidencia» («There is a lack of evidence on learning progress»); Hanushek y Woessmann, «podrían tener un PBI anual en promedio 1,5 % más bajo» («might yield», «an average of 1.5 percent lower annual GDP»); Szpunar, las pruebas intercaladas «se asociaron con menos ansiedad» («was associated with reduced anxiety»).
