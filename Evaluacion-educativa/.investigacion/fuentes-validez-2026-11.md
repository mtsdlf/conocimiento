# Fuentes para los avisos de Validez, confiabilidad y equidad (issue #153)

Cuatro avisos en cuatro notas. Se achicaron tres: confiabilidad, equidad y validez. El de inflación de notas quedó igual, aunque la nota sumó dos estándares. Se corrigieron dos afirmaciones, las dos con `[!WARNING]`: el estándar que trata la oportunidad de aprender (era el 3.19 y el 12.8, no el 13.7) y el nombre de la cuarta inferencia de Kane.

Los extractos en inglés son literales de lo que se leyó; en el informe de Messick, escaneado con OCR, se corrigieron solo errores de reconocimiento evidentes. Los Standards se leyeron del PDF de acceso abierto, extraído a texto con `pdftotext`; el capítulo 3 entero (págs. 49-72) y los pasajes citados de la introducción y de los caps. 1, 2, 12 y 13.

Fuente común: AERA, APA y NCME (2014), *Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing*. https://www.testingstandards.net/uploads/7/6/6/4/76643089/standards_2014edition.pdf. **Texto completo.**

## 1. Equidad y sesgo (`Validez-confiabilidad-y-equidad/equidad-y-sesgo.md`)

**Faltaba**: el capítulo 3 de los Standards leído completo, y evidencia sobre sesgo en evaluaciones de cursos técnicos o con adultos.

**Se encontró**: el capítulo 3, «Fairness in Testing», leído entero. Extractos:

- Varianza irrelevante: «A prime threat to fair and valid interpretation of test scores comes from aspects of the test or testing process that may produce construct-irrelevant variance in scores that systematically lowers or raises scores for identifiable groups of test takers».
- Las cuatro fuentes: las secciones del capítulo se titulan «Test Content», «Test Context», «Test Response» y «Opportunity to Learn».
- Estándar 3.0: «All steps in the testing process, including test design, validation, development, administration, and scoring procedures, should be designed in such a manner as to minimize construct-irrelevant variance».
- Ejemplos (diseño universal): «by avoiding item contexts that would likely be unfamiliar to individuals because of their cultural background, by providing extended administration time when speed is not relevant to the construct being measured, or by minimizing the linguistic load of test items intended to measure constructs other than competencies in the language in which the test is administered».
- Tecnología: «Where technology is involved, it is important that examinees have had similar prior exposure to the technology».
- Rúbricas: «a scoring rubric for a constructed response item might reserve the highest score level for test takers who provide more information or elaboration than was actually requested. In this situation, test takers who simply follow instructions, or test takers who value succinctness in responses, will earn lower scores».
- Diferencias entre grupos: «most testing professionals agree that group differences in testing outcomes should trigger heightened scrutiny for possible sources of test bias»; «However, group differences in outcomes do not in themselves indicate that a testing application is biased or unfair.»
- DIF: «differential item functioning (DIF) is said to occur when equally able test takers differ in their probabilities of answering a test item correctly as a function of group membership»; «The detection of DIF does not always indicate bias in an item; there needs to be a suitable, substantial explanation for the DIF to justify the conclusion that the item is biased.»
- Grupos chicos: «the number of subgroup members in the field test or norming population may limit the possibility of standard empirical analyses»; «potential bias for relevant subgroups may be examined through small-scale tryouts that use cognitive labs and/or interviews or focus groups».
- Estándar 3.20: «When a construct can be measured in different ways that are equal in their degree of construct representation and validity (including freedom from construct-irrelevant variance), test users should consider, among other factors, evidence of subgroup differences in mean scores or in percentages of examinees whose scores exceed the cut scores, in deciding which test and/or cut scores to use.» Comentario: «If the scores from two tests lead to equally valid interpretations and impose similar costs or other burdens, legal considerations may require selecting the test that minimizes subgroup differences.»
- Oportunidad de aprender: «opportunity to learn—the extent to which individuals have had exposure to instruction or knowledge that affords them the opportunity to learn the content and skills targeted by the test». «students' low scores may accurately reflect what they know and can do […] However, it may be considered unfair to severely penalize students for circumstances that are not under their control, that is, for not learning content that their schools have not taught.»
- Estándar 3.19: «In settings where the same authority is responsible for both provision of curriculum and high-stakes decisions based on testing of examinees' curriculum mastery, examinees should not suffer permanent negative consequences if evidence indicates that they have not had the opportunity to learn the test content.»
- Estándar 12.8 (pág. 197): «When test results contribute substantially to decisions about student promotion or graduation, evidence should be provided that students have had an opportunity to learn the content and skills measured by the test.»
- Estándar 13.7 (pág. 212), para la corrección: «When tests are selected for use in evaluation or accountability settings, the ways in which the test results are intended to be used, and the consequences they are expected to promote, should be clearly described, along with cautions against inappropriate uses.» Ni el estándar ni su comentario mencionan la oportunidad de aprender.

**Qué se cambió**: la sección de lo ajeno a lo evaluado cita el capítulo y el estándar 3.0, y cada ejemplo lleva el pasaje de los Standards que lo sostiene; se sumó el de la rúbrica. La sección de diferencias entre grupos cita la definición de DIF y la advertencia de los Standards, la vía para grupos chicos y lo que de verdad dice el 3.20. La sección de oportunidad de aprender tiene un `[!WARNING]`: atribuía la idea al 13.7, y la tratan el 3.19 y el 12.8. En `fuentes:`, los estándares pasaron a 3.0, 3.19, 3.20 y 12.8. El aviso se achicó.

**Sigue faltando**: evidencia de sesgo en cursos técnicos o con adultos. No se buscó en esta ronda; el capítulo 3 no la trae.

## 2. Confiabilidad (`Validez-confiabilidad-y-equidad/confiabilidad.md`)

**Faltaba**: alternativas validadas para estimar la confiabilidad con cohortes chicas, y literatura de medición educativa sobre entrenamiento de correctores.

**Se encontró**:

- Standards, introducción: «In other cases, traditional quantitative analyses may not be feasible due to small sample sizes. However, there may be other methodologies that could be used to gather information to support the standard, such as small sample methodologies, qualitative studies, focus groups, and even logical analysis.» **Texto completo.** No da un método concreto para la confiabilidad.
- Se verificaron las citas que la nota ya tenía. Cap. 2: «we use the term reliability/precision to denote the more general notion of consistency of the scores across instances of the testing procedure»; «the need for precision increases as the consequences of decisions and interpretations grow in importance». Estándar 6.9, comentario: «Procedures should be instituted to calibrate scorers (human or machine) prior to operational scoring, and to monitor how consistently scorers are scoring».
- Jonsson, A. y Svingby, G. (2007), *The Use of Scoring Rubrics: Reliability, Validity and Educational Consequences*, Educational Research Review 2(2):130-144, doi 10.1016/j.edurev.2007.05.002. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ796733. **Abstract de ERIC.**
  - «resulting in a total of 75 studies relevant for this review.»
  - «(1) the reliable scoring of performance assessments can be enhanced by the use of rubrics, especially if they are analytic, topic-specific, and complemented with exemplars and/or rater training».
- Weigle, S. C. (1994), *Using FACETS To Model Rater Training Effects. Draft.* https://eric.ed.gov/?id=ED381994. **Abstract de ERIC** (ERIC tiene el texto completo; no se leyó).
  - «ratings given to English-as-a-Second-Language (ESL) compositions by 8 inexperienced and 8 experienced raters both before and after rater training».
  - «Findings indicate that rater severity evened out somewhat after training across the group. However, the spread of rater severities after training is still quite significant. Rater consistency improved, and new rater extremism was reduced. Results confirm that rater training cannot make raters into duplicates of one another, but it can make them more self-consistent.»

**Qué se cambió**: un párrafo nuevo antes del de psicología del trabajo, con Jonsson y Svingby y Weigle. En «Grupos chicos», un párrafo con lo que dice la introducción de los Standards. Tres fuentes nuevas en `fuentes:`. El aviso se achicó.

**Sigue faltando**: un método validado para estimar la confiabilidad con cohortes chicas, y los dos trabajos leídos enteros. Candidato que se recuerda sin buscar: la teoría de la generalizabilidad (Brennan), que estima varias fuentes de variación a la vez; no se verificó que resuelva el problema de las cohortes chicas.

## 3. Validez (`Validez-confiabilidad-y-equidad/validez.md`)

**Faltaba**: Messick (1995) y Kane (2013) leídos en el original.

**Se encontró**:

- Messick, S. (1994), *Validity of Psychological Assessment: Validation of Inferences from Persons' Responses and Performances as Scientific Inquiry into Score Meaning*, ETS Research Report RR-94-45. https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED380496.pdf. **Texto completo** (33 págs., escaneado con OCR). Tiene el mismo título que el artículo de 1995 y es su versión de informe. Extractos:
  - «Six distinguishable aspects of construct validity are highlighted as a means of addressing central issues implicit in the notion of validity as a unified concept. These are content, substantive, structural, generalizability, external, and consequential aspects of construct validity.»
  - «The substantive aspect refers to theoretical rationales for the observed consistencies in test responses, including process models of task performance (Embretson, 1983), along with empirical evidence that the theoretical processes are actually engaged by respondents in the assessment tasks.»
  - «The consequential aspect appraises the value implications of score interpretation as a basis for action as well as the actual and potential consequences of test use, especially in regard to sources of invalidity related to issues of bias, fairness, and distributive justice».
  - «There are two major threats to construct validity: In the one known as "construct underrepresentation," the assessment is too narrow and fails to include important dimensions or facets of the construct. In the threat to validity known as "construct-irrelevant variance," the assessment is too broad».
  - «Validity is not a property of the test or assessment as such, but rather of the meaning of the test scores.»
- Messick (1995), American Psychologist 50(9):741-749. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ517194. **Abstract de ERIC**: «Six distinguishable aspects of construct validity are highlighted as a means of addressing central issues implicit in the notion of validity as a unified concept.» El texto de la revista no se consiguió: la copia de la Universidad de Bath redirige a la portada.
- Kane, M. T. (2013), *Validating the Interpretations and Uses of Test Scores*, Journal of Educational Measurement 50(1):1-73, doi 10.1111/jedm.12000. https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ996447. **Abstract de ERIC** (no hay texto libre).
  - «Validation then can be thought of as an evaluation of the coherence and completeness of this interpretation/use argument and of the plausibility of its inferences and assumptions.»
  - «Third, more-ambitious claims require more support than less-ambitious claims.»
  - «Sixth, the evaluation of score uses requires an evaluation of the consequences of the proposed uses; negative consequences can render a score use unacceptable.»
  - «Eighth, the validation of the score interpretation on which a score use is based does not validate the score use.»
- Brennan, R. L. (2013), *Commentary on "Validating the Interpretations and Uses of Test Scores"*, Journal of Educational Measurement 50(1). https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ996444. **Abstract de ERIC**: «Kane discusses four types of inferences that provide a scaffolding for addressing these two arguments: scoring, generalization, extrapolation, and decision rules.»
- Standards, cap. 1, para el párrafo de las cinco fuentes: «These sources of evidence may illuminate different aspects of validity, but they do not represent distinct types of validity. Validity is a unitary concept.» Introducción: «the acceptability of a test or test application cannot be determined by using a checklist».

**Qué se cambió**: las amenazas citan a Messick. Los seis aspectos se citan del informe de 1994, con el sustantivo y el consecuencial explicados. Kane se cita por su abstract de ERIC, y las cuatro inferencias, por el abstract de Brennan. Un `[!WARNING]` registra que la cuarta inferencia figuraba como «implicaciones» y Brennan la llama «decision rules». El abstract de Kane no enumera las inferencias, así que no se puede saber qué nombre usa él. La frase «los propios Standards dicen que no lo son [un listado de verificación]» mezclaba dos cosas, y ahora cita las dos por separado. El aviso se achicó.

**Sigue faltando**: Kane (2013) entero y el texto de Messick tal como salió en *American Psychologist*.

## 4. Inflación de notas (`Validez-confiabilidad-y-equidad/inflacion-de-notas.md`)

**Faltaba**: evidencia de inflación de notas en cursos cortos o en capacitación de adultos.

**Se encontró**: nada sobre cursos cortos ni capacitación. Una búsqueda en ERIC («grade inflation» con «adult education», «continuing education», «workplace training», «professional development» o «bootcamp») dio 10 resultados. Se revisaron los seis primeros y ninguno trata de cursos cortos ni de capacitación: son de universidad, community colleges, escuela o enfermería universitaria. Lo que sí apareció, en los Standards, sostiene dos frases de la nota:

- Estándar 12.7, comentario (pág. 197): «When inappropriate test preparation activities occur, such as excessive teaching of items that are equivalent to those on the test, the validity of test score inferences is adversely affected.»
- Estándar 13.8, comentario (págs. 212-213): «Unintended negative consequences may include teaching test items in advance, modifying test administration procedures, and discouraging or excluding certain test takers from taking the test. These practices can lead to spuriously high scores that do not reflect performance on the underlying construct or domain of interest.» «In addition, administering an audit measure (i.e., another measure of the tested construct) may detect possible corruption of scores.»

**Qué se cambió**: un párrafo con el 12.7 y el 13.8 en «Enseñar para la prueba», y la cita de la «audit measure» en el de la señal de inflación. En `fuentes:`, los estándares pasaron a 12.7, 13.7 y 13.8. El aviso quedó igual.

**Sigue faltando**: lo mismo que antes.

## Fuera del lote

- `Evaluacion-educativa/.investigacion/propositos-instrumentos-validez.md`, línea 130, atribuye la oportunidad de aprender al estándar 13.7. Son el 3.19 y el 12.8.
- `Evaluacion-educativa/Fundamentos/fuentes-de-evaluacion-educativa.md`, línea 23, dice que se leyeron los estándares «6.9 y 13.7». Eso es correcto, pero el 13.7 no trata la oportunidad de aprender. Además, ya se leyó el capítulo 3.

## Después del cotejo

Recortes sin fuente nueva: en equidad, las cuatro secciones del capítulo 3 se nombran como secciones (no como «fuentes» que el capítulo enumere), el curso chico va como inferencia, la cita de oportunidad de aprender conserva el «may be considered unfair to severely penalize», y el aviso ya no dice de qué son los ejemplos del capítulo. En confiabilidad, Jonsson y Svingby dicen que la confiabilidad «can be enhanced», no que sea mayor. Los Standards ya no figuran como «texto completo» salvo el capítulo 3: del resto se leyeron los pasajes citados.
