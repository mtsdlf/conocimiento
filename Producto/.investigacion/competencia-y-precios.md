Afirmaciones:

- La cadena de valor de un mercado de consumo "is divided into three parts: suppliers, distributors, and consumers/users". Internet hizo gratis la distribución de bienes digitales y "has made transaction costs zero". (2015)
- Ciclo: lo que atrae más usuarios "attracts the most suppliers, which enhances the user experience in a virtuous cycle". (2015)
- Sobre los proveedores: "suppliers can be commoditized leaving consumers/users as a first order priority"; ejemplo: Google modularizó páginas y artículos. (2015)
- Tres rasgos de un agregador (2017): relación directa con los usuarios ("aggregators have a direct relationship with users"); costo marginal cero para servir usuarios; redes multilaterales impulsadas por la demanda, en las que "customer acquisition costs decrease over time".
- Niveles (2017): "Level 1 Aggregators acquire their supply"; Level 2 "do not own their supply" pero pagan costos de transacción para sumar proveedores; "Level 3 Aggregators do not own their supply and incur no supplier acquisition costs"; "Super-Aggregators operate multi-sided markets with at least three sides".
- En 2017 equipara los términos: "Aggregation Theory describes how platforms (i.e. aggregators) come to dominate the industries in which they compete".
- En 2018 y 2019 los separa. Cita de Gates (relatada por Chamath Palihapitiya): "A platform is when the economic value of everybody that uses it, exceeds the value of the company that creates it." 2019: "Platforms facilitate a relationship between users and 3rd-party developers"; "Aggregators intermediate the relationship between users and 3rd-party developers". En 2018 pone a Facebook y Google como agregadores puros, muy por debajo de esa línea (paráfrasis de la herramienta de lectura).
- DISCREPANCIA interna: en 2017 "platforms (i.e. aggregators)"; desde 2018-2019, plataforma y agregador son cosas distintas.
- Crítica de Taylor (2019): "the old economic rules still work just fine"; "the demand side has become more concentrated than the supply side, thereby giving it more market power"; "'aggregation theory' implies a degree of structural permanence that I do not believe should be automatically assumed".
- Réplica de Thompson: "Aggregators consolidate demand to gain power over supply"; "Walmart stores were big, but finite; Aggregators are infinite". Concede que el artículo original "didn't properly consider the importance of supplier fragmentation" (ejemplo: música en streaming, con proveedores concentrados).
- Wu (2020) [S]: la teoría es "smart, but a little too much Kool-Aid" y puede confundir en materia de antimonopolio; según Marker [S], Thompson respondió que su definición de agregador no incluye a empresas que venden productos físicos, como Apple y Amazon.
- No se encontró crítica académica con revisión por pares a la teoría de la agregación (búsqueda de una ronda).

Recurso canónico: "Defining Aggregators" (2017), los tres rasgos y los niveles; después "A Framework for Regulating Competition on the Internet" (2019) para la diferencia plataforma/agregador. Fijarse en que es un marco de practicante, sin datos sistemáticos.

| Gawer y Henderson (2007), Platform Owner Entry and Innovation in Complementary Markets: Evidence from Intel, JEMS, DOI 10.1111/j.1530-9134.2007.00130.x (abstract vía OpenAlex) | primaria, caso | 3 | 2 | 2 | 2 | [M] |

Nota: el plan pedía "Foerderer et al. 2018 sobre Google y las apps": es el de Google Photos. El de "Wen y Zhu 2019" es de Wen Wen y Feng Zhu.

Afirmaciones:

- Zhu y Liu, abstract: "Platform owners sometimes enter complementors' product spaces and compete against them." Amazon "is more likely to target successful product spaces" y "is less likely to enter product spaces that require greater seller efforts to grow, suggesting that complementors' platform-specific investments influence platform owners' entry decisions". "While Amazon's entry discourages affected third-party sellers from subsequently pursuing growth on the platform, it increases product demand and reduces shipping costs for consumers."
- Zhu y Liu, resumen gerencial: "Many complementors with successful products are pushed out of markets because platform owners enter their product spaces and compete directly with them." Recomiendan "aggregating nonblockbuster products or focusing on products requiring significant platform-specific investments to grow" y "develop capabilities in new product discovery".
- Zhu y Liu, texto: "Amazon enters three percent of complementors' product spaces over a ten-month period, and is more likely to enter the spaces of products with higher sales and better reviews and that do not use Amazon's fulfillment service." Datos en dos rondas, "the first in June 2013, the second in April 2014"; "163,853 products in 22 subcategories" ofrecidos solo por terceros en la primera ronda. Método: propensity-score matching para comparar productos afectados y no afectados.
- Foerderer et al., abstract: usan "Google's 2015 entry into the market for photography apps on its own Android platform as a quasi-experiment"; panel de "6,620 apps"; "Google's entry was associated with a substantial increase in complementary innovation"; "the entry caused a 9.6% increase in the likelihood of major updates for apps affected by Google's entry, compared to similar but not affected apps". Mecanismo: "increased consumer attention for photography apps, instead of competitive 'racing' or 'Red Queen' effects"; el efecto "was particularly pronounced for larger and more diversified complementors".
- Wen y Zhu, abstract: "after Google's entry threat increases, affected developers reduce innovation and raise the prices for the affected apps. However, their incentives to innovate are not completely suppressed; rather, they shift innovation to unaffected and new apps." "Given that many apps already offer similar features, Google's entry threat may thus reduce wasteful development efforts." Resumen gerencial: "platform owners could use direct entry to shape innovation directions and encourage variety of complements"; "The overall welfare implication is thus ambiguous." Diseño (según resumen del buscador [S]): usan casos en que Apple lanzó su app en iOS y Google todavía no en Android, para separar la amenaza de la entrada.
- Kapacinskaite y Mostajabi, abstract: "Generalists experience low repositioning cost and are more likely to shift effort away, while specialists focus their effort on the focal platform." Caso: la app "Files" de Apple: "generalists shift effort toward the competing platform, while specialists double down on the focal platform". "only the platform owner elicits a strong complementor response"; entradas de Microsoft y SanDisk en la App Store sin "a meaningful response by complementors". Hipótesis: los complementadores "with an outside option—those who also operate on a different platform—reposition".
- Gawer y Henderson, abstract: las decisiones de entrada de Intel están moldeadas por la creencia de que "it does not have the capabilities to enter all possible markets", así que "it must encourage widespread entry despite the fact that potential entrants (rationally) fear Intel's ability to 'squeeze' them ex post"; Intel usa estructura y procesos organizacionales "as commitment mechanisms".
- DISCREPANCIA: Zhu y Liu (desalienta a los vendedores afectados) y Wen y Zhu (bajan la innovación en la app afectada) frente a Foerderer et al. (sube la innovación de las apps afectadas, por atención). Contextos distintos: marketplace de productos físicos, amenaza de entrada en Android, entrada real en una categoría de apps.

Recurso canónico: Zhu y Liu (2018), texto libre en DASH de Harvard: introducción, sección de datos y resumen gerencial. Fijarse en qué espacios elige la plataforma (éxito, poca inversión específica del vendedor) y en las recomendaciones a complementadores.

Afirmaciones:

- Tucker: el EVC "is based on the insight that a customer will buy a product only if its value to them outweighs the value of the closest alternative". "to sell a product, a firm needs to price at or below its competitor's price plus the value advantage its product has to the customer over the rival product." Fórmula: Price_a ≤ DifferentiationValue_ab + Price_b.
- Tucker, ejemplo: valor de diferenciación "$6,000" frente a la oferta competitiva más cercana; dos servidores del competidor cuestan "$6,800"; EVC "$6,000 + $6,800 = $12,800".
- Tucker, implementación: "Identify closest competitive offering and calculate its price"; "Be honest about who your closest competitor is" (un servicio B2B de videollamadas cuyo competidor real "is Skype, which is free"); "Be honest with yourself about the price that competitors sell their product for": una empresa de backup calculó un EVC "100 percent greater than what they were charging" pero omitió que "all their rivals discounted at 80 percent from list". "Make sure you define a benefit, not a feature"; "Xerox became very profitable because it priced per photocopy". Usar el EVC como fórmula conviene "When competitor's prices are well-known and concrete". Razones para cobrar por debajo del EVC: "Winner-takes-all market", "Initial Customer Lock-In", "Pre-empt competitive response".
- Wikipedia EVC: método de Forbis y Mehta, 1979 (según McKinsey, no abierto).
- Kalyanaram y Winer: el precio de referencia es "an internal standard against which observed prices are compared". Tres generalizaciones: "consumers use reference prices in making brand choices"; "consumers rely on past prices as part of the reference price formation process"; los consumidores son "more sensitive to 'losses,' i.e. observed prices higher than reference prices, than 'gains.'"
- Mazumdar et al.: revisión de las corrientes conductual y de modelos; marco en tres partes: formación, recuperación y uso, e influencia del precio de referencia.
- Ariely et al.: en seis experimentos, las valoraciones iniciales "are strongly influenced by arbitrary 'anchors'" (a veces "derived from a person's social security number"); el efecto "does not decrease as a result of experience with a good" y "is not necessarily reduced by market forces".
- Rao, Bergen y Davis (bajada de HBR): "Increasingly, price is the weapon of choice—and frequently the skirmishing degenerates into a price war." Según Wikipedia [S], citando a Rao et al.: "Price wars often begin when simple promotional activities are misunderstood as major strategic changes"; ejemplo de las aerolíneas de EE. UU. en 1992, que igualaron y superaron rebajas, con más volumen "but record losses".
- Heil y Helsen [S, vía Wikipedia]: entre las condiciones de una guerra de precios, "a primary focus on competitors rather than consumers".
- van Heerde et al., abstract: guerra de precios en supermercados holandeses desde el otoño de 2003, iniciada por el líder "to halt its sliding market share"; panel de "1821 households", dos años antes y dos después. El gasto por visita "ultimately dropped"; los consumidores quedaron "more sensitive to weekly prices and price image"; el iniciador frenó la caída de su cuota y "its stock price improved"; "The losers were the rival mid-level and high-end chains."
- Nagle, Müller y Gruyaert (7.ª ed.), índice: capítulo 6 "Price Competition". Descripción del editor: en vez de "calculating prices to cover costs or to achieve sales goals", el lector aprende a influir en la percepción de valor. Según el buscador [S], en la 6.ª ed. es el cap. 7 "Price Competition: Managing Conflict Thoughtfully", y en la 5.ª el cap. 11 "Competition"; el capítulo describe una brecha de competencia de precios cuando la empresa decide "without anticipating" la respuesta de los competidores [S].
- Armstrong y Collopy (1996), abstract: laboratorio con "1016 subjects made pricing decisions"; con información de los beneficios del rival, "over 40% of the subjects were willing to sacrifice part of their company's profits to beat or harm the competitor". Ya desarrollado en Estrategia-competitiva/Analisis-de-competidores/obsesion-por-el-competidor.md.

Recurso canónico: Tucker, MIT OCW 15.818, notas de la clase 2 (siete páginas, libres): la fórmula y la lista de implementación. Para profundidad, Nagle, Müller y Gruyaert, cap. 6 de la 7.ª ed. (no leído).
